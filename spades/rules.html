♠ Spades rules

The standard rules, which the score tracker uses by default. Many groups play with house rules; you can change the scoring values on the tracker’s setup screen under Rules.

Players and goal

  • 4 players in 2 teams of 2. Partners sit across from each other.
  • A standard 52-card deck, no jokers. Cards rank from Ace (high) down to 2.
  • Spades are always trump.
  • The first team to reach 500 points wins.

Dealing

  • Deal all 13 cards to each player, one at a time.
  • The deal passes to the left after each round.

Bidding

  • Starting left of the dealer, each player bids how many tricks they expect to win, from 0 to 13.
  • Partners’ bids are added together. That total is the team’s bid: the number of tricks the team must win together.
  • Nil: a player can bid nil, promising to win no tricks at all. Their partner still makes their own bid.
  • Blind nil (optional): a nil bid made before looking at your cards. It’s riskier, so it’s worth double.

Playing tricks

  • The player left of the dealer leads the first trick. Play goes clockwise.
  • You must follow suit if you can. If you can’t, you may play any card, including a spade.
  • The trick is won by the highest spade played. If no spade was played, the highest card of the suit that was led wins.
  • The winner of a trick leads the next one.
  • Spades can’t be led until they’re broken (a spade has been played on an earlier trick), unless the leader has nothing but spades.

Scoring

Team wins at least as many tricks as it bid+10 per trick bid
Each extra trick over the bid (a “bag”)+1
Team wins fewer tricks than it bid (“set”)−10 per trick bid
Every 10 bags a team collects−100
Nil made (player won no tricks)+100
Nil failed (player won any trick)−100
Blind nil made / failed+200 / −200

Bags carry over from round to round. When a team reaches 10, it loses 100 points and any bags over 10 carry on toward the next penalty.

If a nil bidder wins tricks, those tricks still count toward their partner’s bid.

Example

A team bids 4 + 3 = 7 and wins 9 tricks: 70 points for the bid, plus 2 bags, for 72. If they had won only 6 tricks, they’d score −70.

Winning and losing

  • The first team to reach 500 wins. If both teams pass 500 in the same round, the higher score wins.
  • A team that drops to −200 or below loses.
